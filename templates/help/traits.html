Traits and Trait Sets

Cortex Prime games can use very different Trait Sets. The names on your sheet may be Attributes, Skills, Distinctions, Values, Relationships, Powers, Roles, Stress, or something else entirely. The controls described here work the same way regardless of what your GM calls the Trait Set.

Die Traits

A die Trait has a permanent die rating such as d6, d8, or d10. Click the die itself to change its permanent value.

The Add to Dice Pool control adds the Trait to your current Dice Pool and opens the Dice Pool window. If the GM has configured an image for the Trait, that image may appear beside the Trait or may replace the normal Add to Dice Pool control.

Temporary Step Up and Step Down

The small controls beneath a die change its effective value without changing the permanent rating:

  • Minus: temporarily step the Trait down.
  • Reset: remove the temporary adjustment and return to the permanent value.
  • Plus: temporarily step the Trait up.

Temporary adjustments are useful for scene effects, injuries, bonuses, penalties, or any other rule that changes a Trait for a limited time. Rolls and saved Dice Pool Macros use the Trait's current effective value.

The reset control near the top of the actor sheet clears all temporary die adjustments on that Actor at once.

Other Trait Value Types

The module supports several ordinary Trait value types. Which ones appear on your sheet depends on GM configuration.

  • Die: a Cortex die rating used in rolls.
  • Number: a numeric value that can be edited directly.
  • Text: a freeform text value.
  • Resource: a tracked current value with increment/decrement controls.

Resource Traits

Resource Traits track a current numeric amount. The GM may configure minimum, maximum, starting value, and step size. Resource images may be decorative or may replace the consume/decrement control.

Descriptions, Descriptors, SFX, and Sub-Traits

Trait Sets can enable additional information beneath each Trait. Depending on the Actor Type, a Trait may have a description, descriptors, SFX, or nested Sub-Traits.

Sub-Traits can be diceless or can have dice if the GM enables dice for them. This makes them useful for specialties, focused abilities, tags, or any other nested Trait structure your Cortex build needs.

Custom Traits

A GM can allow players to create custom Traits inside selected Trait Sets. When allowed, use the Add Custom Trait control in that Trait Set.

Custom Traits belong to that Actor and can be deleted normally. GMs may create custom Traits even when player creation is disabled.

Hiding and Restoring Inherited Traits

The eye-slash control hides an inherited Trait on the current Actor. This is not the same as deleting the Trait from the Actor Type.

A hidden inherited Trait:

  • disappears from that Actor's normal sheet;
  • does not change the Actor Type or other Actors;
  • stays hidden through Actor Type synchronization;
  • keeps its actor-local value and temporary step state;
  • can be restored later from the Trait Set's Hidden Traits list.

Assets and Complications

Assets and Complications use their own specialized Add controls. They are actor-local entries and can be removed when they are no longer relevant.

Their exact game effects are determined by your Cortex Prime rules and your GM; the sheet provides tracking and dice interaction rather than enforcing one universal interpretation.

Notes

Actor Types may enable a Notes tab. Notes are actor-local entries intended for information that does not need to be represented as a Trait.
